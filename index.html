I'm an M.S. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at UC San Diego, working with Prof. Johannes Schöneberg. I develop AI for biology and drug discovery: machine learning models that read how cells respond to drugs from microscopy images, and that keep working when the lab, batch or donor changes.

My work so far spans two projects. As first author, I showed that a small image model taught by two teachers of different modality, raw pixels and CellProfiler profiles, can outperform frozen models up to 14× its size, including Cell Painting foundation models, at retrieving a compound's effect across laboratories. As co-first author, I trained a self-supervised model on live-cell images of mitochondria from 144,624 human fibroblasts, without age or drug labels: it reads a donor's age group from mitochondria alone, defines an aging axis, and reveals that drugs act differently on adult and neonatal cells.

Next, I want to go beyond images. A cell's response to a drug is measured in many modalities, including imaging, gene expression, proteomics and chemical structure, and each captures a different part of it. In my PhD, I want to build AI that learns across these modalities, so that what is measured in one can inform another, and use it to help discover new therapies.

I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.
